**Did you know?** In Saint Lucia, during a divorce or annulment, the Court has the authority to decide how property is divided. This includes both community and separate property, and contributions such as money, services or management can influence the final order.

## Understanding property division

When a marriage ends, one of the most complex and emotionally charged issues is the division of property. The Court’s role is to make this process fair and equitable. It can include assets acquired during the marriage (community property) and those owned individually by each spouse (separate property).

## What the Court considers

1. **Type of property.** The Court distinguishes between community property, which is jointly owned, and separate property, which belongs to one spouse.
2. **Contributions.** Contributions by each spouse, whether financial, through services or through management, are considered. Non-monetary contributions, like homemaking or child-rearing, can affect the division.
3. **Fairness and equity.** The Court aims for a fair and equitable distribution, taking into account the needs and circumstances of both parties.

## Community and separate property

- **Community property.** Assets acquired during the marriage, such as homes, cars and savings, are typically community property and subject to division by the Court.
- **Separate property.** Assets one spouse owned before the marriage or acquired individually, such as inheritances or gifts, are usually separate property. If they have been mixed with community property, they may be subject to division.

## Contributions matter

Financial contributions are straightforward, but homemaking, child-rearing and managing household affairs count too, and can significantly influence the Court’s decision.

## Why this matters

Understanding how property is divided helps set realistic expectations and prepare for the proceedings, whether you are dealing with community or separate property.
